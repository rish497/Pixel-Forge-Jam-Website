04 / The rules

Keep it fair.
Make it yours.

The guardrails are simple: begin during the jam, respect other creators, and be honest about what went into your submission.

Start during the jam

Previously started games cannot be submitted.

Solo or teams up to four

Use any engine and divide the work however you like.

Credit pre-made assets

Existing assets are permitted when their licenses allow it and they are properly credited.

No generative AI

AI-generated code, art, audio, writing, and other generated content are banned.

Keep it appropriate

No hateful, offensive, or NSFW content. Disclose horror, violence, and sensitive material.

Make it accessible

Browser builds are recommended. Windows and macOS downloads are accepted.

Play fair

No rating manipulation. Give other participants respectful, useful feedback.

Ship what you have

Unfinished games are welcome. A two-day upload window follows the jam for bug fixes.

08 / Questions

Good to
know.

Who can participate?

Anyone interested in making games, from complete beginners to experienced developers. Work alone or with up to three teammates.

Do I keep ownership of my game?

Yes. You retain ownership. By submitting, you allow Pixel Forge to feature the game in event coverage on its website, YouTube, and social channels.

Can I use pre-made assets?

Yes, provided their licenses allow it and they are properly credited. The game itself must begin during the jam.

What if my game is unfinished?

Submit it anyway. Experimental, unfinished, and buggy projects are welcome.

Who runs Pixel Forge?

Pixel Forge is a student-run game development community fiscally sponsored by The Hack Foundation, doing business as Hack Club, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit.
